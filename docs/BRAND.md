# Socado — Brand Guidelines

This document is the source of truth for Socado’s brand identity, visual direction, tone of voice, and digital brand application.

It should be consulted by:

* Designers.
* Frontend developers.
* Content creators.
* AI agents.
* Product teams.
* External partners producing digital experiences for Socado.

Its purpose is to maintain a coherent, recognizable, and authentic Socado experience across every touchpoint.

---

## 1. Brand essence

Socado’s central philosophy is expressed through three pillars:

```text
Social. Coffee. Connection.
```

Socado is not presented only as a place to consume coffee.

The brand represents a space where:

* People meet.
* Coffee creates moments.
* Experiences create connection.
* Everyday moments can feel special.
* Quality and warmth coexist.

The brand must maintain a thoughtful balance between emotional and rational expression.

It should communicate warmth and sensitivity while also expressing clarity, confidence, and intention.

---

## 2. Brand personality

Socado’s personality is organized around three official principles.

### “Simplemente Elegante”

The experience should feel:

* Minimal.
* Transparent.
* Authentic.
* Clear.
* Refined.
* Free from unnecessary elements.

Socado’s elegance does not come from adding more decoration.

It comes from choosing each element carefully and giving it a clear purpose.

Avoid:

* Over-design.
* Visual saturation.
* Decoration without purpose.
* Excessive messaging.
* Interactions that compete with content.
* Effects that distract from the experience.
* Unnecessary visual complexity.

### “Cálidamente Conectado”

The experience should feel:

* Welcoming.
* Human.
* Approachable.
* Intelligent.
* Inspiring.
* Inclusive.

The brand should create empathy without losing sophistication.

Communication should support a natural relationship between people, coffee, physical spaces, products, and the Socado experience.

### “Únicamente Sobrio”

The experience should feel:

* Discreet.
* Modern.
* Direct.
* Open.
* Balanced.
* Confident.

Sobriety must not be confused with coldness.

Visual resources may be contemporary and expressive, but they must remain controlled and coherent.

---

## 3. Design principles

Every Socado experience should follow these principles:

1. Communicate before decorating.
2. Maintain a clear visual hierarchy.
3. Use space as an active part of the design.
4. Avoid elements that compete with one another.
5. Prioritize authentic and understandable content.
6. Maintain consistency in color, typography, imagery, and components.
7. Create warmth without becoming overly informal.
8. Create sophistication without becoming distant.
9. Use movement and visual effects with restraint.
10. Preserve the official identity across every touchpoint.
11. Let photography and content carry the experience.
12. Prefer clarity when choosing between clarity and visual novelty.

## 6. Copywriting rules

When writing for Socado:

* Use clear and direct sentences.
* Maintain a warm expression.
* Avoid unnecessarily long paragraphs.
* Explain information simply.
* Use natural calls to action.
* Avoid excessive commercial pressure.
* Avoid exaggerated promises.
* Avoid forced humor.
* Avoid generic phrases that could belong to any brand.
* Maintain consistency in product, service, and category names.
* Prioritize user benefits and experiences.
* Use uppercase text with moderation.
* Preserve approved user-facing copy unless a change is explicitly requested.
* Avoid translating official product or campaign names without approval.

Copy should support the experience rather than dominate it.

## 8. Logo usage

### Authorized versions

Only official available versions of the following may be used:

* Logotype.
* Imagotype.
* Isotype.
* Social identifier.
* Market identifier.
* Kitchen identifier.

Do not recreate any version manually.

### Clear space

The exclusion area around the logo is defined by the proportion of the letter “O” within the official logotype.

The clear-space area must not contain:

* Text.
* Icons.
* High-contrast photographic elements.
* Borders.
* Buttons.
* Other logos.
* Decorative elements.

The logo must always retain enough space to preserve its legibility and presence.

### Authorized logo colors

The logo may only be displayed in:

* Azul Socado.
* White.
* Ivory.

The background must belong to the approved palette and provide sufficient contrast.

### Prohibited logo treatments

Never:

* Recreate the logo.
* Redraw the logo.
* Modify its proportions.
* Stretch it.
* Compress it.
* Rotate it.
* Tilt it.
* Distort it.
* Change its composition.
* Separate elements without an official variation.
* Apply unauthorized colors.
* Add shadows.
* Add glossy reflections.
* Apply bevels.
* Apply three-dimensional effects.
* Add unofficial outlines.
* Place it over a background that reduces legibility.
* Animate its internal parts in a way that alters the identity.

When logo animation is explicitly approved, the animation must preserve its shape, proportions, spacing, and composition.

## 10. Typography

Socado’s typographic system uses Raleway and Outfit.

### Primary typeface: Raleway

Authorized weights:

* Regular.
* Medium.
* Semi-Bold.
* Bold.

Primary uses:

* Headings.
* Subheadings.
* Highlighted messages.
* High-hierarchy navigation.
* Primary calls to action.
* Editorial elements.

Raleway should communicate Socado’s refined and contemporary character.

### Secondary typeface: Outfit

Authorized weights:

* Light.
* Regular.
* Semi-Bold.

Primary uses:

* Body copy.
* Descriptions.
* Long-form text.
* Supporting subtitles.
* Labels.
* Numbers.
* Data.
* Functional information.

Outfit should provide clear, approachable, and modern readability.

---

## 11. Text hierarchy and alignment

Typography hierarchy must remain clear and consistent.

General rules:

* Primary headings use Raleway.
* Numbers uses Outfit.
* Headings must be clearly distinguished from supporting content.
* Weight variations should be preferred over introducing additional typefaces.
* Text blocks should retain adequate visual space.
* The number of typographic styles should remain controlled.
* Existing approved sizes and line breaks must not be changed during motion-only tasks.

Text should be left-aligned by default.

Centered or right-aligned text should only be used when supported by a clear compositional reason.

Primary headings may occasionally use uppercase text, especially in:

* Hero sections.
* Editorial sections.
* High-impact messages.
* Specific campaigns.

Uppercase text must not be overused, particularly in body copy or functional messages.

## 13. Color distribution

The 85/15 ratio is a general guideline:

* Approximately 85% primary colors.
* Approximately 15% secondary colors.

This does not mean that every page must use every color.

A valid composition may use only:

* Ivory.
* Azul Socado.
* One controlled accent color.

The priority is clarity and coherence.

Secondary colors must complement the identity rather than compete with it.

---

## 14. Contrast and readability

Every color combination must maintain clear readability.

Pay particular attention to:

* Text over Terra.
* Text over Celeste Socado.
* Text over photography.
* Buttons over dark backgrounds.
* Disabled states.
* Error messages.
* Small labels.
* Interactive elements.
* Focus indicators.

When an approved color combination does not provide sufficient contrast for a specific use, select another approved combination.

Do not modify official colors arbitrarily to solve a contrast problem.

The intended accessibility target is WCAG 2.1 AA whenever reasonably achievable.

## 15. Digital implementation

For digital interfaces:

* Use the existing design tokens.
* Use named Tailwind utilities instead of raw hexadecimal values.
* Maintain consistent palette usage.
* Maintain a controlled typographic hierarchy.
* Prioritize clarity over decoration.
* Use photography and spacing as part of the experience.
* Avoid exaggerated shadows.
* Avoid unjustified gradients.
* Avoid unnecessary three-dimensional effects.
* Maintain visual consistency between components.
* Design hover, focus, active, loading, and disabled states within the brand language.
* Preserve keyboard navigation.
* Preserve visible focus states.
* Respect reduced-motion preferences.
* Avoid creating a second token system outside the established frontend tokens.

The implemented design tokens live in:

```text
src/app/(frontend)/styles.css
```

Practical frontend implementation rules belong in:

```text
docs/UI_UX.md
```

## 16. Photography and imagery

Photography should communicate:

* Product quality.
* Warmth.
* Human connection.
* Authentic moments.
* Contemporary hospitality.
* Care and attention to detail.

Images should feel intentional rather than decorative.

Avoid:

* Generic stock photography that does not feel connected to Socado.
* Excessively staged imagery.
* Artificial or heavily processed food.
* Visually noisy backgrounds that compromise content.
* Crops that remove the central purpose of the image.
* Unapproved filters that alter brand colors.

Image dimensions, sources, crops, and aspect ratios must not be changed during motion-only tasks unless explicitly requested.

---

## 17. Motion and animation

Motion should reflect Socado’s personality:

* Elegant.
* Warm.
* Controlled.
* Natural.
* Contemporary.
* Subtle.
* Cohesive.

Animations should:

* Support comprehension.
* Guide attention.
* Create continuity.
* Reinforce hierarchy.
* Maintain a fluid experience.
* Preserve approved layout geometry.
* Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

Avoid:

* Exaggerated bouncing.
* Aggressive movement.
* Strong parallax.
* Unnecessary decorative rotation.
* Effects that reduce readability.
* Animations that distort the logo.
* Excessively long entrances.
* Unrelated motion styles across sections.
* Effects that make the experience feel childish.
* Motion that blocks access to content.
* Animating layout properties.
* Hiding essential content until JavaScript is ready.

Detailed motion decisions should be recorded in:

```text
docs/motion/MOTION_MAP.md
```

## 20. Approval criteria

A design, component, animation, or piece of content is aligned with Socado when:

* It feels warm without becoming overly informal.
* It feels elegant without becoming distant.
* It feels modern without relying entirely on temporary trends.
* It communicates clearly.
* It preserves visual hierarchy.
* It uses the official palette.
* It uses Raleway and Outfit correctly.
* It preserves the logo.
* It avoids over-design.
* It supports a natural connection with the Socado experience.
* It remains usable and accessible.

When choosing between a decorative solution and a clear solution, choose the clear solution.
